- Drawers go together the way a woodworker builds them. A drawer bottom
  sits in grooves in the sides, front and back and grows into each, or
  slides in under a back that stops on top of it. A groove can take its
  width from your cutter, and the checks catch one too deep, too near an
  edge, too narrow for its panel or loose on it. A rabbet can go two
  thirds of its part's thickness deep, as a drawer corner's does, and the
  new dado and rabbet hooks a drawer front into its sides. Claude builds
  drawer boxes this way, and never screws a bottom on underneath.
